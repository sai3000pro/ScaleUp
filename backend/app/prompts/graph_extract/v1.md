You are building a prerequisite graph of *skills* from one window of a textbook.

A skill is something a learner can be asked to **demonstrate**. "Chapter 3" is
not a skill. "Computing a partial derivative" is. If you cannot imagine writing a
short-answer question about it, it is not a skill — or it is one with
`assessable: false`.

## Slugs are the merge key

Windows are extracted independently and merged afterwards by slug. Two windows
describing the same idea must produce the **same** slug, so:

- Use the conventional name for the concept, not this author's phrasing. Prefer
  `gradient-descent` over `the-method-of-steepest-descent-as-described-here`.
- Name the concept, never the chapter or the section.
- Lowercase kebab-case, at most 64 characters.

## Rules

- At most **12** concepts. Without a cap you will produce one per paragraph; a
  window of six chunks rarely contains more than a handful of genuine skills.
- `summary` is at most 240 characters and says what the learner can *do*.
- `difficulty` is 1–5 relative to this book, not to mathematics in general.
- `assessable: false` for concepts that carry structure but have too little
  substance to question — a section that only names a thing, for instance.
- `evidence_ordinals` cites the chunk ordinals below that support the concept.

## Prerequisites

Emit a prerequisite only when you can defend it from **this window's text**.
Prerequisites that span windows are handled by a later pass; do not guess at them.

`A -> B` means: a learner who does not understand A **cannot meaningfully
attempt** B. It does not mean A and B are related, adjacent, or usually taught
in that order. Set `confidence` honestly — a low number is more useful than a
confident guess, because low-confidence edges lose ties and get logged.

Never emit `A -> A`. Never emit both `A -> B` and `B -> A`.

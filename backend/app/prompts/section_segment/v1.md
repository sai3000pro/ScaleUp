Name the concepts a section of a textbook introduces.

The section below has already been cut into fragments **by the book itself** —
each one begins at a line the author wrote to announce a new item (`Definition`,
`Theorem: Farka's Lemma`, `Remark 1`, `Weak Duality Theorem`). Worked examples
and proofs have already been folded into the item they illustrate.

**The fragments are fixed. Your job is to name and summarise them, not to decide
where they begin or end.** Return exactly one object per `[fragment N]` you are
given, using that same `index`. Do not invent a fragment, do not split one, and
do not reorder them.

## Titles

`title` is the name of the thing the fragment **defines or states**, as the book
names it: "Subgradient", "Slater point", "Weak Duality Theorem", "Sublevel sets
of a convex function are convex".

- Take the wording from the fragment. If the text says "s is a *subgradient* of
  f at x̄", the title is "Subgradient".
- Never return a lead-in label. "Definition", "Remark", "Remark 1", "Theorem"
  and "Example" name nothing and are rejected.
- Never return the section's own title unchanged; that node already exists.
- A fragment that states a *result* about something is titled by the result, not
  by its subject: "Local optima are global on convex sets", not "Convexity".
- Keep it under about eight words. It is a node in a skill tree, not a sentence.

## Summaries

`summary` says what a learner must be able to do to have this skill — state the
definition, prove the result, apply the procedure — in one or two sentences
drawn from the fragment's own content. Do not add material the fragment does not
contain, and do not restate the title.

`key_terms` are up to six terms the fragment introduces or relies on. Omit it
rather than padding it.

## Folding

Set `standalone: false` when a fragment is **not a separate skill** — when it
merely continues, restates, or completes the fragment immediately above it. It
is then merged into that fragment, and the title and summary you gave it are
discarded.

Use this sparingly and only in the clear cases. It is the one change to the
segmentation you are allowed to request, and it can only ever join fragments —
there is no way to ask for a new boundary, because the book already decided
where the boundaries are.

Everything else gets `standalone: true`, including a fragment that looks minor.
A short definition is still a definition.

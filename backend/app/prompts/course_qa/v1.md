Answer a learner's question about this course, **from the passages below and

The passages were retrieved from the learner's own uploaded material. Each one
carries the skill-tree node it belongs to, so an answer that cites its passages
also points at the part of the tree to go and study.

## The rule that matters

Every claim in `answer` must be supported by a passage, and every passage you
lean on must appear in `citations` with a `quote` **copied verbatim from that
passage**. Not a paraphrase, not a sentence you compose — a substring of the text
printed below. If you cannot quote it, you do not have it, and you must not
assert it.

This is the same discipline the prerequisite pass is held to. It exists because
an answer that is right about the subject but not about *this book* is worse than
no answer: the learner has no way to tell the two apart, and the citation is the
only thing that makes the difference checkable.

## When the passages do not cover the question

Say so plainly in `answer` and return `"citations": []`. "The retrieved material
does not cover X" is a correct and useful answer. Filling the gap from general
knowledge is not, however confident you are — the learner is being tested on this
book.

## Style

- Answer the question first, in two to five sentences. No preamble.
- Prefer the book's own vocabulary, so the answer matches what the learner will
  be drilled on.
- Do not mention passage numbers, node ids, or "the retrieved text" in `answer`.
  The citations carry that; the prose should read like an explanation.

## Identifiers

`node_id` and `chunk_id` must be copied **exactly** as printed in the passage
header. A citation naming anything else is discarded, and the claim it was
supporting then stands unsupported.

Find which of the sections below **depend on** one particular skill.

You are given one **source skill**, with the text where the book defines it, and
a numbered set of **candidate sections**, each with an excerpt of its own prose.
For each candidate, decide one thing: *does this section use the source skill?*

This is the mirror of the usual question. A section rarely names the foundation
it is standing on — it does not write "Inverse of a Matrix", it writes "since
`A_B` is invertible". Reading the foundation's definition first is what lets you
recognise its notation, its objects, and its results when they turn up in
someone else's prose.

## What counts as a dependency

The source skill `S` is a prerequisite of candidate `C` when a learner who has
not met `S` **cannot follow C's excerpt**. The test is dependency of
understanding, not order of presentation.

Evidence that a dependency is real:

- C's excerpt uses the notation, object, or result that S introduces — even
  under a different name, and especially under a different name.
- C's procedure has a step that *is* S.
- C is stated as a generalisation, special case, or correction of S.

Things that are **not** dependencies, and are the most common mistake here:

- **C comes later in the book than S.** Reading order is not dependency.
- C and S are about related subject matter, or share generic vocabulary
  ("matrix", "solution", "problem", "set") that the whole book uses.
- S is "foundational" in a general sense but C never actually uses it.

Most candidates in any batch will not depend on the source. Returning two or
three out of twenty is a normal answer, and `"dependents": []` is a real answer.
Do not fill quota — you are shown a batch because batching is cheap, not because
the batch is expected to contain hits.

## Direction

You never state a direction. The source is the prerequisite and every slug you
return is a thing that needs it. If you find yourself wanting to say the source
depends on a candidate, that edge is not yours to emit: leave it out.

## Confidence and evidence

`confidence` is how strongly *the candidate's excerpt* supports the dependency:
above 0.8 when the excerpt uses the source's own notation or names its result,
around 0.7 when it is clearly relying on it in other words, below 0.6 when you
are inferring from topic alone — and those are dropped, so prefer to omit them.

`evidence` must be a phrase **quoted from that candidate's excerpt**, not from
the source text above it and not a sentence you compose. If you cannot quote
something from the candidate itself, you do not have evidence and should not
name it.

`target_slug` must be one of the candidate slugs exactly as written. A slug that
was not shown to you in this batch is discarded, so inventing one wastes the
edge.

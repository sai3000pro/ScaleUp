## What counts as a prerequisite

`A` is a prerequisite of `B` when a learner who has not met `A` **cannot follow
B's explanation**. The test is dependency of understanding, not order of
presentation.

Evidence that a dependency is real:

- B's text uses a term, object, or result that A introduces — "recall that",
  "by the simplex method", "since the matrix is invertible".
- B's procedure has a step that *is* A.
- B is stated as a generalisation, special case, or correction of A.

Things that are **not** prerequisites, and are the most common mistake here:

- **A comes earlier in the book.** Reading order is not dependency. Two chapters
  can be studied in either order and often are.
- A and B are about related subject matter, or share vocabulary.
- A is "foundational" in a general sense but is never actually used by B.

If the section genuinely depends on nothing in the list, return `"edges": []`.
An empty answer is a real and common answer. Do not fill quota.

## Direction

`prereq_slug` is the thing needed **first**. `target_slug` is the section you
are reading. Getting this backwards inverts the tree, and a cycle it creates
will be rejected and thrown away — so check the direction on every edge.

Do not emit an edge whose `prereq_slug` and `target_slug` are the same, and do
not restate the parent–child nesting already given by the table of contents.

## Confidence and evidence

`confidence` is how strongly *the text* supports the dependency: above 0.8 when
the section names the prerequisite outright, around 0.6 when it is clearly
relying on it without naming it, below 0.5 when you are inferring from topic
alone — and those are dropped, so prefer to omit them.

`evidence` must be a phrase **quoted from the section text below**, not a
sentence you compose. If you cannot quote something, you do not have evidence
and should not emit the edge.

Write one short-answer question that tests whether a learner has actually
understood the skill below, using only the source material provided.

## The question

- Answerable in two or three sentences. Not an essay, not a single word.
- Tests **understanding**, not recall of a phrase. "State the definition of X" is
  a bad question; "why does X fail when Y" is a good one.
- Answerable from the source material alone. Do not require facts that are not
  there.
- Do not mention "the text", "the passage", or "the chapter" — the learner is
  being asked about the subject, not about a document.

## The rubric

Two to four points. Each is one idea a correct answer must convey, phrased so a
grader can judge **meaning rather than wording** — a learner who says the right
thing in their own words must score full marks.

Weights sum to 1.0. Put more weight on the load-bearing idea than on the
peripheral one.

Set `difficulty` 1–5 for the question you actually wrote, which may differ from
the skill's own difficulty.

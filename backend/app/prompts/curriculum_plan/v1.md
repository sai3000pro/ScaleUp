Musical instruments overlap far more than they differ. Reading a quarter note,
keeping a pulse, orienting yourself on the instrument and shaping a phrase are the
same skills whether the learner is holding a guitar, a banjo or a cello. Every one
of those belongs in the tree as a catalogue skill, referenced by its `id` in a
`from` field, so that a learner of two instruments is learning the same skill twice
rather than two skills that happen to share a name.

Write a concept as inline (no `from`) ONLY when it is genuinely particular to this
instrument and no catalogue skill covers it — bowing, embouchure, limb independence,
a specific tuning. If a catalogue skill nearly covers it, use the catalogue skill and
restate its wording instead.

Rules you must follow:

- Every `from` must be an exact `id` from the catalogue above. Never invent one.
- A catalogue-drawn concept may restate only `slug`, `title`, `summary`, `difficulty`
  and `key_terms`. Nothing else. Restate the title and summary in this instrument's
  own vocabulary — "keep the pulse with the bow" rather than "keep the pulse".
- An inline concept must declare `title`, `summary` and `difficulty`.
- Every `slug` must be unique within this tree, lowercase, hyphenated, and prefixed
  with the instrument where that reads naturally (`cello-pulse`, not `pulse`).
- Between 6 and 14 concepts. Fewer is not a curriculum; more is a syllabus nobody
  finishes.
- Every edge's `prereq` and `target` must be slugs you defined in `concepts`.
- Order the tree the way this instrument is actually taught. The catalogue's
  `suggested_order` is a starting point, not a constraint — if this instrument
  genuinely needs something earlier or later, say so and give a `rationale`.
- Start the tree with one or two concepts that have no prerequisites, so the learner
  has somewhere to begin.
